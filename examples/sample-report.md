**Purpose:** Help the head of operations approve or reject a bounded experiment
**Audience:** Head of operations and three team leads
**Stage:** First decision-ready draft
**Stakes:** Wasted time, unnecessary spending, and a premature company-wide rollout
**Quality bar:** A feasible two-week pilot, a specific decision request, and a way to judge improvement

## Bottom line

**needs-work** — The core idea is concrete, but this draft asks for the wrong commitment. Make it an existing-tools pilot with a decision request and a success criterion before sending.

## Prioritized changes

### 1. A company-wide purchase is not the pilot the brief calls for (F1)
**high impact · goal · high confidence**

  - **A1 · L8** (quote): I propose buying a new collaboration tool and rolling it out to every team.

**Why it matters:** The head of operations must either reject the spending or reopen scope before considering the meeting experiment.
**Change:** Propose a two-week experiment with one team using the current shared document. Keep any software purchase out of this decision.
**Check improvement:** Can the reader approve the pilot without approving new spend or a wider rollout?

### 2. The final line defers the decision (F2)
**high impact · goal · high confidence**

  - **A1 · L11** (quote): Let's discuss this next week.

**Why it matters:** The intended next action is pilot approval; the close creates another meeting instead of making that choice legible.
**Change:** Close with an explicit approve-or-reject request and state which owner and team still need confirmation.
**Check improvement:** Ask a reader what decision is being requested. They should identify the pilot, not another discussion.

### 3. The proposal cannot distinguish a shorter meeting from a better meeting (F3)
**medium impact · goal · medium confidence**

  - **A1 · L5** (quote): The meeting feels slow. Several people have complained.

**Why it matters:** Subjective frustration establishes a reason to investigate, not whether cutting meeting time preserves decision quality.
**Change:** Before the pilot, establish a baseline and propose tracking both time and missed or deferred decisions. Do not invent measurements.
**Check improvement:** After two weeks, compare time and decision quality before recommending broader adoption.

## Preserve
