# Fundraising / persuasive decks

Adapt to audience, fundraising stage, and intended next action. A first-meeting
deck is not a diligence data room. A sales deck is not an investor deck.

Lenses to consider, not a mandatory checklist:
- Can the audience restate the pain, customer, and proposed solution?
- Is the strongest credible evidence early enough to affect belief?
- Does the market argument explain a reachable wedge, rather than only a big TAM?
- Is "why now" a mechanism, not just a fashionable trend?
- Are traction, projections, and assumptions visibly distinguished?
- Does team evidence connect to the hard parts of the problem?
- Is the ask proportionate, with a plausible milestone or use of funds?
- Does the sequence build conviction instead of repeating slogans?

Anchors:
Weak: abstract pain, unsupported superlatives, no specific next step.
Competent: legible story and ask, but central assumptions still weakly supported.
Strong: a specific customer and wedge, stage-appropriate proof, coherent sequence,
and a credible next step. Strength is not a demand for mature revenue at pre-seed.

## Annotated synthetic example
Weak opening: "A $100B opportunity. We are revolutionizing construction."
It names neither a customer nor a mechanism; a big market cannot carry the thesis.
Stronger direction: "Site managers reconcile subcontractor updates by hand. We
turn existing updates into one daily exception list."
This makes the customer, workflow, and wedge inspectable. It is NOT proof of demand;
a real deck must supply its own stage-appropriate evidence. Never copy invented
traction or metrics into the work. At exploration stage, suggest testing this story;
at fundraising stage, identify which claims the actual deck supports.

Never fabricate traction, competitors, quotes, or market facts. Suggest what
evidence to obtain, not made-up evidence to put on a slide. PDF text cannot
establish visual quality. For layout, inspect supplied slide screenshots.
