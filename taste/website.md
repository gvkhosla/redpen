# Websites / visual product work

Adapt to the visitor's job and the site's purpose. A portfolio, checkout, and
enterprise landing page have different standards. Distinctiveness and restraint
are contextual: avoid imposing one aesthetic on every task.

Lenses to consider:
- What does a newcomer understand from the first screen?
- Is the next action clear, credible, and appropriate to commitment level?
- Does hierarchy guide attention toward the intended outcome?
- Do proof and imagery support the claims or just decorate them?
- Is the design recognizably suited to this audience, or competent but generic?
- Are copy, visual hierarchy, and interaction expectations aligned?
- For exploration: which direction has promise, and what should be tested?
- For launch: are responsive behavior, accessibility, and critical flows verified?

Weak: unclear audience or value, competing priorities, unsupported promises.
Competent: understandable, consistent, but potentially forgettable or unconvincing.
Strong: clear audience fit, distinctive expression, credible proof, deliberate
hierarchy, and low-friction next action.

## Annotated synthetic example
Weak headline: "The future of productivity, powered by AI."
A newcomer cannot tell whether this is for architects, students, or sales teams.
Stronger direction: "Weekly project updates, without chasing every architect."
For operations leads at architecture firms, this names a recognizable job and pain.
It still needs honest support and a suitable next action; sharper copy is not proof
of conversion. For messaging exploration, compare comprehension with real readers.
For launch, inspect the rendered hierarchy and separately test the actual flow.
Neither example dictates a visual style or invents customer evidence.

A screenshot supports visual critique, not verified usability, keyboard
accessibility, contrast measurements, conversion rates, or mobile behavior at
other widths. HTML source is not a rendered page. Mark these as unverified and
recommend browser or human tests; do not report those tests as performed.
